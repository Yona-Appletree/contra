The debugger as a move-review instrument

Round 3. Two modes, a dance and a figure, and one flat screen for each: hairlines, no boxes in boxes. The text is beside the picture, its problems under it. Pixels are always 2×. The 3d is always below the pixels, dragged to turn, with buttons for the angles that matter. Figure mode is the harness for the loop that comes next — an agent edits the figure's text, Yona looks: the contexts it is judged in are rig lines in that text, one card each.

“too many boxes in boxes … two different modes, figure and dances … the small cards should be when in figure mode, showing it in different contexts/transitions. the source code should define them … in dance mode the whole dance on top in 16-bit, the 3d view below … problems in the bottom left below the code, timeline and graphs under the main content … 3d should support dragging … presets for common angles.”

1  A dance — the whole hall on top, the 3d below it

Six sets and the two couples out, all at 2× in one strip. Click a set and the 3d turns to it alone; hall brings the whole line back. ⟲ loops the running call; ⏮ ⏭ jump call by call. The deck under the 3d folds to its tab row with ▾.

2  A figure — a card per context, the clicked one in 3d

The figure's own file: its body as pre, body, post, then one rig line per context it must look right in — an end, the other side, the calls it comes out of and goes into, a shorter count, nobody across. Every rig is a card; the tape runs the clicked card's clock, transitions included; ⏮ ⏭ step between cards. Add a rig line and a card appears; change a beats and every card re-times on the keystroke.

3  What the mock is not

Every call begins and ends in becket lines, so any one of them loops alone and any two abut; the hey is a spline through four waypoints, not a hey; the figure's pre/body/post is read for its line numbers only, the motion is still the mock's own; the problems are written by hand and inject a defect puts one on the screen so it can be seen carried to its line, its beat and its card. What is real: both texts are parsed live, the calls and rigs and their beats come out of them, and the 3d is drawn from the same bodies the pixels are.
