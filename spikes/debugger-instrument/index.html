The debugger as a move-review instrument

One dance, four layouts of the same debugger. The engine underneath every picture is the same mock: Butter, eight moves, sixty-four beats, one minor set, drawn the way the Stage draws — integer pixels on the hall's black floor, role colours, the robin's hand on top. The question is not what the pixels show; it is what a screen has to put beside them so a move can be judged in one look, and what has to get out of the way.

“the pixels are squished and zoomed oddly … timeline horizontal … code more prominent … I don't really care about the graphs right now, maybe as a tab … transport that jumps move by move … a loop-move button … one move outside a dance, in several test rigs … nothing but my eyes seem able to judge the quality of a move.”

1  What it is now — the eight panes, equal weight, the pixels a quarter of a quarter

A faithful mock of today's grid: three strips, then eight panes at 4×2. The pixels are one cell of eight and take the shape the cell gives them; the code is a cell of eight too. Everything below is an argument with this screen.

now — packages/kinetics/debugger

2  A — pixels centre, code left, timeline below — everything else on the rail

The hall takes the middle at a real integer zoom and never changes size. The .dance text is a full column, lit at the running call. Under both, the horizontal timeline. Graphs, tree, listing, layout and 3d are a vertical rail on the right that slides a drawer over the hall when you want one.

A — one hall, one column of text, one ruler

3  B — pixels and code split, the timeline a full-width strip — tabs beneath it

Text and hall get half the width each, so a long call line is readable without scrolling. The timeline is a strip the full width of the screen, and the tab row under it is where the graphs, the tree, the listing, the layout and the 3d view live — one at a time, collapsible to nothing.

B — half and half, one strip, one tab row

4  C — the rigs are the home page — the dance is a mode you switch into

You open the debugger to judge a move, not a dance. So the home page is one move in four contexts at once, looping, with the move list on the left and the call's own text on the right. The whole dance is behind the dance switch, and lands wherever the move was.

C — move first, dance second

5  One move, four rigs — the page the other two get to through a mode

The same call run in the four contexts it will meet: the middle of a long set, the top end with nobody above, the bottom end, and the other side of the set. All four on the same loop, so the eye compares them on the same beat. A rig that complains says so on its own caption — the strip below is the four rigs' complaints together.

rigs — one call, four contexts, one loop

Judge this one first. If four rigs at this size is not enough picture, the whole idea wants a bigger cell and fewer rigs.

6  What the mock is not

Every move here begins and ends in becket lines, so any move can be looped alone — the real engine's geometry is the real engine's. The hey is a spline through four waypoints, not a hey. The complaints are written by hand, and the defect switch in the strip above injects one so the strip, the ruler and the rig captions can be seen carrying bad news. What is real: the text is butter.dance, the moves and their beats are parsed out of it live, and editing a beats = n or deleting a call re-times the dance in every picture on this page at once.
