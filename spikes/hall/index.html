Hands Four · the hall

UX spike for the simulator's look (vision: "The hall, rendered"; D13, D14, D15, D20, D21; Q4, Q5, Q6). Round 2 after the first gate. Rendering contract: a fixed-size low-res world sized by the number of lines, shown at an integer zoom; positions held at 1/256 px; sprites and arm lines blitted at whole pixels; anti-aliasing allowed on the low-res grid for traces and shadows. Round-2 rulings applied: FF6-size people (24 px, real faces) and chibi dropped; LttP inward walls, grange floor and shadows as defaults; trails persist for a whole dance; arms are puppets: the body sprite has no arms, each hand is a point on the floor that both dancers compute from the same keyframe, and the arm is a two-segment line from the shoulder, so joined hands meet by construction in both renderers. Everything animated is canned keyframes for one 64-beat dance, not an engine; a small seam pass eases hands and facing across figure boundaries.

0. The hall (the D21 test)

Press Play music to hear the tune; the animation clock then ticks off the audio clock. The hall is a fixed world: two lines by default (phone-sized at 1×), add a line and the hall grows. Waiting-out couples at each end hold hands, cross over during the last eight beats and re-enter.

1. View angle, with puppet arms

Same minor set, same keyframes, same clock, at 3×. Both renderers draw arms from the shoulder to a shared hand point, so watch whether the hands stay joined through the balance, the swing, the ring and the twirl. The front view is the one at risk: hands that reach "forward" in floor space become arms pointing up or down the screen.

1a · front-drawn on a top-down floor

Shoulders sit on the sprite; the hand point is the floor point lifted by a hand height (waist for lines and rings, chest for swings, over the head for the twirl arch).

1b · true overhead

Body drawn per angle (32 steps), arms are lines to the floor point, elbows bend outward when there is slack.

2. People (FF6-size, 24 px)

Composed from parts: skin, hair style and colour, shirt, trousers or skirt, height, cap. Columns are facings clockwise from S. Rows: the 8-facing set standing; then at 16 facings: stand · walk A · walk B · balance rock (lean, weight on the right foot, hands forward and low) · hands joined to the sides (long lines) · hands up (twirl) · swing hold (left hand out, right hand round the partner). Arms in the gallery use fixed hand points in sprite space.

3. Traces on the floor

One cycle of the four dancers of a minor set. Worn: every pass darkens the boards. Per dancer: four colours (larks blue, robins rose, ones darker). By figure family: colours from the card taxonomy. The 24-px glyphs are the grid thumbnail candidates. The hall above uses per-dancer trails that persist for a full dance.
