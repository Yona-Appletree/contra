Hands Four · the hall

UX spike for the simulator's look (vision: "The hall, rendered"; D13, D14, D15, D20, D21; Q4, Q5, Q6). Thesis: a 16-bit hall drawn from above with front-drawn people, procedurally composed from parts, moving on the beat of a real tune, reads as a contra dance and not as a diagram. Rendering contract: a real low-resolution mode (resolution derived from the container and an integer zoom), positions held internally at 1/256 px, sprites blitted only at whole low-res pixels, no rotated or sub-pixel-moving sprites; anti-aliasing is allowed on the low-res grid (traces, shadows) as if a strong sprite co-processor drew them. Everything animated here is canned keyframes for one 64-beat dance, not an engine.

Leans going in: front-drawn people on a top-down floor (Q5); FF6-style tall people over LttP chibi (Yona's stated preference); FF6 facade walls; 8 facings for front-drawn sprites, 16 free for overhead; procedural composition with an outline pass rather than a hand-drawn sheet (Q4); worn-board traces in the hall and family-coloured glyphs for the grid; ABC via abcjs with the AudioContext as the master clock (Q6). Defaults on load reflect these leans; every alternative is one click away. Options can also be set in the URL, e.g. ?proto=chibi&walls=lttp&scale=3.

0. The hall (the D21 test)

On load the hall should already feel like an overhead video of a dance. Press Play music to hear the tune (a user gesture is required for audio; the tune joins at the current beat, and the animation clock then ticks off the audio clock). Two sets of unequal length, a waiting-out couple at each end who cross over and re-enter, a band on the stage, a caller at the mic, people sitting out on the sides.

1. View angle

Same minor set, same keyframes, same clock. Left: floor from above, characters drawn from the front (the Link to the Past / FF6 convention). Right: true overhead, heads and shoulders only. Both keep trace geometry honest because the trace is drawn at the feet; the question is which reads as people. Traces are drawn under both so the geometry can be compared directly.

1a · front-drawn on a top-down floor

Faces, arms, skirts. The feet are the floor position; the body stands "up" out of the map.

1b · true overhead

Rotation is free (16 facings), lean and arms show as head and hand offsets, but no faces.

1c · 3/4 squash (rejected)

Floor foreshortened 0.6 in y. Looks like a game room, but circles become ellipses and traces stop being a plan view.

2. People

Procedurally composed from parts (skin, hair style and colour, shirt, trousers or skirt, height, cap) with fixed poses. Columns are facings (S first, clockwise on screen). Rows: stand · walk A · walk B · balance rock (lean forward, weight on the right foot, arms trailing) · arms out · arms up (twirl) · swing hold. The 16-facing row differs from the 8-facing row only in head turn; judge whether that is worth doubling the pose set. Robot test: the crowd row below is 18 random people standing still; if they read as a crowd the composition is enough.

2a · FF6-style tall (16 rows)

Head 5 wide with a 3-wide face, 3-wide torso, 1-px legs. Taller, thinner, more adult.

2b · LttP-style chibi (13 rows)

Head is half the height, 2-px eyes, stubby legs. Cuter, more toy-like.

2c · walk cycle and balance, animated on the beat

Left to right: walking S, W, N, NE; balancing (rock on 1, back on 3); twirling; swinging pair. Tall then chibi.

2d · hand-pixelled sprite vs composed

A hand-drawn 12×18 sprite with 2-tone shading (stand, walk, walk, balance) beside the composed tall person with outline. The gap is shading and silhouette, which composition can add as a pass.

3. Traces on the floor

One cycle of the four dancers of a minor set. Worn: every pass darkens the boards, so the swing is a dark disc and the lines are faint. Chalk per dancer: four colours, direction readable. By figure family: colours from the card taxonomy (swing rose, circle green, lines blue, allemande gold, twirl purple). The 24-px glyphs are the grid thumbnail candidates.

5. Beat indicator, driven by the tune

The tune is an ABC transcription of Soldier's Joy rendered and synthesised by abcjs (MIT). AABB in the tune is A1 A2 B1 B2 in the dance. Four candidate indicators, all reading the same clock: a 64-cell strip (also under the hall), a card-style readout with the current figure filling, the notation with the current bar highlighted, and a lamp.
