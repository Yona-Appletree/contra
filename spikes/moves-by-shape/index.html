The Moves page by shape · figures in context

Thesis. The Moves page today files 45 figures by the shape kind of their definition — balances, turns for two, rings and stars, walks. That is the library's filing system, not a dancer's. A dancer asks "I am standing in a line of four facing down the hall — what happens now?", and the answer is a different cut of the same 45. This spike puts the formation first and asks which of four ways of doing that a caller would actually browse.

The user, 2026-09-15. “it may make sense to organize the page with formation as the first item of the hierarchy. like, ‘hands-four’ or contra set moves whatever we call the default arrangement. then alternate forms, like short wavy lines, long wavy lines, down the set, diamond”
“some moves only make sense in bigger sets (promenade the set, long lines) or different configurations (turn as a couple or alone in short lines down the hall) and seeing the full dance as an example might be good, too.”
“I don’t see the turn alone or turn as couple or the other couple ways you can turn to come back up in context… something to think about, putting in the common combinations”
(on turn alone) “this is almost always done in a short line of four after going down the hall. so it should probably be shown that way?” — (on contra corners) “can only be shown correctly with 6 dancers”

How to read this. Section 0 is the decision matrix: all four concepts in 390 px phone frames, side by side, scrolling independently. Sections 1–4 are the same four concepts at full desktop width, each with its own controls, so you can actually use them. Every picture is a real committed strip from apps/web/e2e/strips/ and every dance plot a real traces/dances/*-strip.svg, copied in — nothing here is drawn by hand and nothing imports production code. Tiles loop through their strip's frames on the app's own clock; pause them with the control strip if a screenshot is wanted.

One honest caveat, because it changes what you are judging. The strips that exist were rendered over each figure's own group — the formation named under every tile (duple-improper, proper, becket). Since FR-B1 the tile machinery can draw a figure over any set, so in the build each tile would be re-rendered in the shape of its section. Where a real picture of a figure in a non-default shape already exists — the turn alone into a diamond, the box circulate in long waves, the wave of four, contra corners over four couples — this spike uses it and says so with a gold in shape chip. Judge the arrangement, and read a grey default set chip as "this picture will be redrawn".

0. The four concepts, side by side, at 390 px

The same 45 figures, the same 21 dances, the same pictures — four ways in. Each frame scrolls on its own; they share one clock and one selection, so picking a figure in B and a dance in C shows in the full-width sections too. On a phone these stack; on a desk the row scrolls sideways.

1. Concept A — By shape: the page is the formations

The idea. The top level of the page is the arrangement the set is standing in, in the user's own order: the lines (a hands four) first, then the alternate forms. Each section opens with a diagram of the shape — where the four (or eight) stand and which way they look — then the figures that are danced from it, each drawn in it, then the common combinations that live in that shape. A figure that is danced from three shapes appears in three sections; that is the point, not a duplication bug. Families are gone from the top level; every tile still carries its family as a chip.

What it costs. The longest page of the four, and the lines section is a third of the library, so the default arrangement is still a wall. What it buys: it is the only one of the four where turn alone is filed under "a line of four, down the hall" and a reader meets it there, which is exactly what the review asked for.

2. Concept B — The figure in context: one move, every shape it lives in

The idea. Browsing stays as it is; the row grows. Open a figure and it shows itself in every shape it appears in — one strip per shape, with a sentence on what changes — then the common combinations it belongs to as short sequences with the real seam strips behind a disclosure, then the dances that call it. This is the answer to "I don't see the turn alone… in context": a figure page carries its own context instead of the page's filing carrying it.

What it costs. Nothing organises the browse — you have to already know which move you want. What it buys: it is the deepest answer per move, and the only concept that can say "almost always danced in a short line of four after going down the hall" on the move itself.

3. Concept C — The dance as the example: pick a dance, watch it in order

The idea. "Seeing the full dance as an example might be good, too." A dance opens as its own strip — the real traces/dances/<slug>-strip.svg, one small pen plot per call, the cell as wide as the call is long — and then every call in order with its own looping tile, its count, the caller's words, and the shape the set is standing in beside it. Every call opens that figure's page (concept B).

What it costs. It is a dance browser, not a move browser: a figure only appears where some dance calls it, so the nine figures no dance calls have no home. What it buys: context for free and honestly — the combination is not a curated list, it is the record.

4. Concept D — Shape × family as facets: one grid, two keys, plus the review

The idea. No editorial order at all. A filter bar carries the shapes, the families and the review status from the register, and a dense grid of tiles reflows under it; a group by switch decides which key is the heading and which is the filter, so the same page is "by shape, families as a filter" or "by family, shapes as a filter" depending on which way you came in. This is the concept that does double duty as the review surface the register asked for ("the Moves page could show each row's status so the review is done on the page").

What it costs. A tool, not a page: no prose, no combinations, nothing to read on a first visit. What it buys: it is the only one that answers "show me everything still unreviewed that is danced from a wave" in one gesture, and the only one that can be re-keyed without a rebuild.

5. The gate

Where the pictures come from. pix/figures/ — all 45 committed figure strips. pix/seams/ — 21 committed seam strips, which are what makes a "common combination" a real picture rather than a caption. pix/variants/ — 14 committed parameter-row strips, which is where the genuine in-shape pictures live (the turn alone into a diamond, the wave of four left-first, the circulate with the robins facing in). pix/dances/ — six real dance plots. All copied in; the spike imports nothing (check:deps forbids it).
The shape a figure is filed under is the spike's own reading. The model knows five shapes (set/shape.ts: lines, line-of-four, wave, ring, diamond) and only four facts about which figure makes which: down-the-hall and up-the-hall end in a line of four, balance-wave ends in a wave, bend-the-line ends in a ring, and Jeremy Corners' two turn-alones land in a diamond. Those five are marked from the model. Everything else on this page is a proposed filing, marked spike's reading, and is exactly what the gate is for — the shape list and the assignment are the decision, the layout is the easier half.
