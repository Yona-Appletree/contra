Move motion · the beat in a walk

Thesis. Most walking figures in the library ease their whole travel as one curve — long-lines is a single cosine over eight beats, walkStep (pass through, right and left through, the chain's pull-by) one smoothstep over the whole walk, ringWalk (circle, star) one smoothstep over the turn, and petronella, roll away, the twirl and wait-out the same. The moves that already feel right — swing, allemande, do-si-do — run on a constant-speed trapezoid instead: up to speed in about a beat, cruise, down in about a beat. So the question is not "add bounce": contra glides, and the contract says so (feet ±2.6 px, sway 1.5°, no vertical motion). The question is where the beat lives when the body glides — in the speed profile, in a small per-beat pulse, in the count sheet, or in the feet — and how little of it is enough.

How to read this. Every panel loops the same figure on one clock. Turn the click on and watch whether the dancer's steps land on it. The graph under each panel is the honest view: distance along the path (gold) and speed (grey) against the beat grid; the dots are where the body is on each count. Ghosts stamp the torso on every whole beat so a screenshot shows beat-locking. Section 3 lays the same half-beats side by side, which is what a phone review reads.

1. Where the body is on each count — five body-path models

Same minor set, same figure, same clock, same feet. Only the function from beat to floor position differs. 1a is what ships. 1b is the smallest possible change: the same tween on a clock that hesitates on every beat, one line at the sampling layer, no figure rewritten. 1c is the profile the swing already uses. 1d is 1c on the hesitating clock. 1e reads a count sheet — long lines is three steps and a touch, not four steps — and interpolates between the counts, so the dancer arrives on 3 and stands on 4. The pulse-depth slider drives 1b and 1d: 0 is no pulse at all, 1 is a dead stop on every beat, which is what slide-left's stepped() does today.

2. Where the beat lives when the body glides — two feet models

One dancer at 8×, walking the figure's own path (default: the cruise profile of 1c, so the body is smooth and only the feet differ). 2a is today's quiet motion: both feet oscillate ±2.6 px in the body's own frame, scaled by speed, so at walking pace both shoes slide along the boards with the body. 2b plants: a foot lands on the beat and stays fixed to the floor while the body glides over it, then swings to its next plant in the last half of the beat. From above a planted shoe is the strongest step cue there is, and it costs the body path nothing. The sway is the same 1.5° in both.

4. Dial it in

One set at 6× with every knob: body path, feet, pulse depth, ramp length for the cruise, and the click. This is where "a little beat, not much" gets a number. The readout says the peak speed as a multiple of the average, which is the one figure that separates a glide from a march: the shipped tween peaks at 1.5× (smoothstep) or 1.57× (cosine); a cruise with one-beat ramps peaks at 1.33× on a four-beat leg; a dead stop on every beat is 1.5× again but four times over.
