The Stage · transport, card, mute

UX spike for the front page, 2026-09-16. Three asks: standard playback controls (to the start of the dance / of the move, play, next move, next dance) with one-press and two-press meanings; the dance as a real card whose moves you can tap to jump to and open for details, with a link through to the Moves page; and mute in the top-right of the stage, music on by default, remembered in localStorage, untouched by a change of dance. Everything below runs on one clock: press play anywhere and every concept moves together. The hall is a still of the real canvas (the caller's bubble is left off it), the tune is a stand-in for abcjs.

Glyph key. A bar means "to a boundary", one triangle is a move, two are a dance: |◀◀ start of the dance (again: the previous dance) · |◀ start of this move (again: the previous move) · ▶ / ▮▮ · ▶| next move · ▶▶| next dance. Keyboard: space play, ←/→ moves, ⇧←/⇧→ dances, m mute.

Where I'd push back, and what the strip lets you compare:
  1. "Two presses quickly" vs the CD-player rule. A timed double-press has to hold the first press back ~300 ms to see if a second is coming, so the one control that should snap gets a lag; and the timing is invisible. Every music player instead does restart, or previous if you're already near the start — and pressing twice quickly then also goes back (first press restarts, second finds you at the start). Both rules are wired up; the strip switches them. Lean: the CD rule, threshold two beats.
  2. What "the beginning" of a dance is. The shipped reset goes to the line-up (the announcement, the walk, hands four, then the potatoes — half a minute of silent hall). A transport's |◀◀ wants the potatoes: four beats before the first call, band counting in. The line-up stays what the evening does between dances on its own. Both wired up; lean: potatoes.
  3. Mute is not pause. Today the speaker is play/pause. Once mute is its own thing, "muted and playing" has to keep the tune's clock running at gain 0 — the beat stays a linear function of AudioContext.currentTime (AC4) — and the very first ▶ still has to be the click that primes the audio. Mute is a preference, not a state of the evening, so it shouldn't spill onto the Tunes jukebox: its own key, hall:muted.
  4. The phone's control row is already full. Five 44 px buttons don't fit beside the dance select and the tempo slider, so it's two rows: transport, then settings. Concept 1C keeps one row by putting the transport on the stage, at the cost of covering the bottom-left chairs.

1. The transport

Where the five controls live, on a phone. Each frame is a 390 × 844 viewport that scrolls, so the question "is play above the fold, and is the card?" is answered by looking. The one-line readout under each transport says what the last press did.

2. The card

Four cards at the laptop's card width (420 px). A is what ships. In B–D tapping a call jumps the evening to it (paused stays paused, on the move's first beat); the small ⓘ opens the move: the dance's own call, its parameters, the short teach, the long one folded, and the links through to the Moves page. Pick Fatal Attraction in the strip for the awkward cases — a two-beat call, a concurrent "‖" line, and long calls.

3. Mute, top-right of the stage

The top-right corner is where the band sits, so the button lands on the piano. Two sizes: the 44 px chip the stage's other buttons use, and a 32 px one that clears more of the band. Click either to mute; the strip's storage readout shows what was written. "Change dance" below the frames and "reload page" in the strip are the two things that must not touch it.

4. Put together

My lean: 1B (a transport bar under the stage) + 2B (chips) + the 32 px mute top-right. Phone on the left with the move opening as a bottom sheet; laptop on the right with the same move opening as a popover beside the card. The dance select, tempo and trails stay where they are, on a second row.
